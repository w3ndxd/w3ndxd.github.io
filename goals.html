Goals and SWOT analysis

My professional goal

Within four years of starting I want to work as a software engineer who builds reliable software in a small technical team. The route is on my Why IT page, and my direction page shows which skills I need and how they match my SWOT. Below, my SWOT leads to two SMART goals and a mission for this year, the first steps toward this professional goal.

My SWOT as a student

Strengths

  • Strong in math: 37 of 40 questions correct in the Turkish state exam, vwo maths B passed
  • I learn fast: Dutch B2 within five months
  • I plan and work structured
  • I connect easily with people

Weaknesses

  • I check my work more than twice, so I spend too long on small details
  • I am still a beginner in programming
  • I sometimes procrastinate and start assignments too late

Opportunities

  • Help from teachers, teacher assistants and my student career coach
  • Learning from other students in code reviews
  • The first years are broad, so I can try both tracks before I choose

Threats

  • Skipping my planning in a busy week
  • Losing motivation when my code does not work
  • Social media and social activities taking too much time

My two SMART goals

In the first year you need 60 EC for your propedeuse, and the Study Advice boundary is 45 EC. That is about 15 EC per quartile.

Goal 1
(strength: I plan and work every day)

I'm going to use my planning habit to earn 15 EC in every quartile, so I have all 60 EC at the end of this year.

Specific and measurable: every week I set my priorities and fixed study times in my calendar, and I check my EC in Osiris after every grade.

Time-bound: I check my progress at the end of every quartile. If I fail an exam, I make a resit plan within a week.
Goal 2
(weakness: too long on details)

I'm going to stop over-checking: I check an assignment twice at most and then hand it in.

Specific and measurable: I set a timer for my final check and write down after every assignment whether I handed it in on time.

Time-bound: I evaluate this after the first quartile and keep it if I met every deadline.

What I need to pass my first year

  • A 5.5 or higher on every test, because that is the minimum score for each test in the Implementation Regulations (IR).
  • 60 EC at the end of the year, and at least 45 EC to stay above the Study Advice boundary.
  • Registering for every test in Osiris before the deadline in the second week of each block. I have two attempts per test in an academic year, so a failed test means a resit plan right away.
  • Checking my EC in Osiris after every grade, so I see in time how many EC I can still lose.

My mission this year

This year I am turning my daily planning into results: 15 EC in every quartile and all 60 EC by the end of the year. I am also learning to trust my work, so I check an assignment twice at most and hand it in on time. I ask teachers and classmates for help early instead of getting stuck on details. By the end of the year I want proof that a good plan and a calm finish beat perfection.
